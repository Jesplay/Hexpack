# Block Mimicry

> **Category:** Patterns › [[Spells (Category)|Spells]]  
> **Source mod:** [[Hexical]]

The Art of **Hexcasting** is a versatile craft; I can use **[[Media|media]]** to simulate a lot of the actions that I rely on blocks to perform without requiring any physical infrastructure. With just my [[Staves|staff]], I can [[Cut Stone|cut stone into stairs]], [[Dispense|launch arrows]], and [[Cook|cook]] my items for me.

Tales speak of ancient **Hexcasters** that have set up entire magical factories without placing a single block.

*And I need a bigger office, I need a bigger chair*
*A bigger desk, a bigger staff*
*A bigger hat to wear*

`vector, vector` → `—`

Takes a block position and an axis vector and pushes the block along that vector. Costs an eighth of an [[Amethyst|Amethyst Dust]].

Similar to the block it is mimicking, this spell can push chains of up to 12 blocks if needed and break fragile blocks that a regular **Piston** would break. It also respects the stickiness of blocks like **Slime** and **Honey**, as well as the immovability of certain blocks like **Obsidian**.

`item entity, vector, vector` → `—`

Takes an item entity, a position, and an axis vector and dispenses the item. For most applications, costs about half of one [[Amethyst|Amethyst Dust]].

More specifically, this spell acts as though a dispenser suddenly appears with the item loaded and fires the dispenser.

If this spell is used for any type of **Arrow**, the price increases to a full [[Amethyst|Charged Amethyst]]. I wonder why Nature would have such an adverse reaction to this specific application. Perhaps I would be too powerful?

*Upon request, I can also perform a hug.*

`item entity, identifier` → `—`

Takes an item entity and cuts the item into the shape of an item identifier. Costs about an eighth of one [[Amethyst|Amethyst Dust]].

The target item must be possible to carve from the source item in a **Stonecutter**. For instance, **Stone** can be cut into all manners of stairs, slabs, and walls.

*I saw the angel in the marble and carved until I set him free.*
